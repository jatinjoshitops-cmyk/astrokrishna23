Tantra, Mantra & Yantra — Traditional Remedial Guidance

Tantra, Mantra and Yantra are three of the oldest remedial branches of Vedic practice. At Krishna Astro each one is matched to what your Kundli actually shows, and explained in plain language before you're asked to follow anything.

What Tantra, Mantra and Yantra Actually Mean

These three words get used loosely, and often sensationally, so it's worth separating them. A mantra is a sound formula recited a set number of times, usually tied to a particular planet or deity, and usually at a particular time of day. A yantra is a geometric diagram — engraved on copper or drawn on paper — that acts as a focus for that same energy, often kept at home or at a place of work. Tantra, in this remedial sense, refers to the structured method that governs how such practices are performed: the sequence, the timing, and the discipline around them.

None of the three is a shortcut, and none of them replaces your own effort or judgement. In traditional Jyotish they are supportive measures — intended to steady a difficult planetary period, not to override anyone's will or guarantee a particular outcome. Krishna Astro is direct about that from the first conversation, because the alternative is letting you expect something the practice was never meant to deliver.

Why the Kundli Comes First

A mantra or yantra suggested without reading your chart is guesswork. The same remedy that suits one person's Saturn period can be entirely irrelevant to someone else's. So every consultation begins with your date, time and place of birth, and the remedy — if one is suggested at all — follows from what the chart shows about the specific problem you've described.

Sometimes the honest answer is that no remedy is needed, and the situation is better addressed practically. That gets said too.

How a Yantra Is Usually Used at Home

Where a yantra is suggested, the guidance covers the practical side: which direction it should face, where in the home or workplace it belongs, how it should be kept clean, and how often it needs attention. These details matter more than most people expect, and a yantra placed carelessly is generally treated as no yantra at all.

Yantras are normally inexpensive and widely available, and you'll be told plainly what to look for so you aren't overpaying for something ordinary. If a particular yantra isn't practical for your living situation — a shared home, a rented flat, limited space — that's worked around rather than insisted upon.

Photo: a yantra placed at a home altar

Mantra Practice: What It Actually Involves

A mantra remedy usually comes with three specifics: which mantra, how many repetitions, and when. The count and timing are not decorative — they're part of the traditional method, and following them loosely tends to defeat the point. For most people the daily commitment is modest, a few minutes at a fixed time.

If you're not comfortable with Sanskrit pronunciation, that's extremely common and easily handled — the mantra is explained slowly, and a simpler alternative can be suggested where one exists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is tantra safe, or is it something to be cautious about?

The remedial practice offered here is limited to mantra recitation, yantra placement and the traditional method around them. Nothing is suggested that involves harm to anyone, and every step is explained before you agree to it.

What is the difference between a mantra and a yantra?

A mantra is a sound formula recited a set number of times; a yantra is a geometric diagram kept at home or work as a focus for the same influence. They're often suggested together but either can be used alone.

Do I need to know Sanskrit to follow a mantra remedy?

No. Pronunciation is explained slowly, and where a simpler alternative exists it can be suggested instead.

How much time does a mantra practice take each day?

For most remedies, a few minutes at a fixed time. The specific count and timing are part of the method and are explained clearly.

Are yantras expensive?

Generally no. You'll be told what to look for and roughly what it should cost, so you aren't overpaying for something widely available.

Can I use a yantra in a rented or shared home?

Yes. Placement guidance is adapted to your actual living situation rather than assuming a dedicated pooja room.

How is this different from the Vashikaran service?

Vashikaran focuses specifically on communication and resistance within an existing relationship. This page covers the wider remedial toolkit of mantra, yantra and traditional method.

Will you guarantee a result?

No. Remedies are offered as traditional supportive guidance, not as a guaranteed outcome or a fixed timeline, and that is said plainly at the start.

Can this be done without visiting in person?

Yes. Phone and WhatsApp consultations work the same way, since the reading is based on your birth details.

What if my chart doesn't call for any remedy?

Then that's what you'll be told. Some situations are better addressed practically, and saying so is part of an honest consultation.
